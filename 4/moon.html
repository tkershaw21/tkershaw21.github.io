About the Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. Its bright highlands and dark plains record a long history of impacts and volcanic activity. The darker areas, called maria, formed when lava filled large basins.

The Moon rotates at the same rate that it orbits Earth, so we generally see the same face. Its phases occur because our view of its sunlit portion changes during the orbit, not because Earth normally blocks the sunlight.

Interesting fact

Some permanently shadowed craters near the Moon's poles contain water ice.
